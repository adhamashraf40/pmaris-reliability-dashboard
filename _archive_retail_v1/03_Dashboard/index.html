The peaks and troughs are not seasonality. The fitted month-on-month trend is +0.46% of an average month, and the lag-1 autocorrelation is -0.47 — each month tends to sit opposite the one before it, which is what independent draws look like, not a season. October's high and September's low are the same phenomenon: twelve numbers scattered around one flat average.

Read the totals, not the order. Revenue by category returns chi-square p = 0.57 and revenue by city p = 0.72; the gap between the highest and lowest recorded city is matched by chance 35% of the time. Every ranking above is inside the range random variation produces, so no bar on this page is an instruction.

The single point of this dashboard: this file reports activity accurately and supports no prioritisation. The totals, the transaction count and the calendar coverage are real and can be quoted. The ordering of categories, cities, months and customer segments cannot — every test run against them comes back empty. A dashboard that ranked them anyway would be inventing the one thing the data does not contain.
