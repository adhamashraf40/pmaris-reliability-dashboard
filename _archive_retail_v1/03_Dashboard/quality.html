This dataset is technically flawless and analytically empty.

The finding of the quality review, stated plainly.

Every cleaning check a data engineer would run came back clean on the first pass. One thousand rows, nine columns, no missing values, no disguised nulls, no duplicates, no header damage, no impossible dates, no outliers to trim. Nothing needed repairing, so nothing was repaired. As a file, it arrives in better shape than most production exports.

Then it was tested for meaning, and it failed every test. Revenue does not vary by category, by city, by gender or by their interaction beyond what chance produces at this sample size. The twelve-month trend is flat. Revenue is uncorrelated with the quantity sold. The leading digits of the revenue column do not follow Benford's law — they follow a flat random draw. The file is clean because it was generated, and being generated is exactly why it cannot answer a business question.

Eight cleaning checks, zero defects

Run before any analysis. Every check returned a clean result, so no rows were dropped, imputed or corrected.

Seven tests of meaning, zero signals

Run after cleaning, live from the data contract. A verdict of "no signal" means the observed spread is what random variation produces at 1,000 rows.

Three columns that do not mean what they are named

These are not dirty values. Each value is internally valid; it is the relationship the column name promises that is absent.

Leading digit of every revenue value, against Benford's law

Genuine financial amounts follow Benford's law. These do not: chi-square 442, p = 2e-90. The values match a flat random draw between 101 and 4,995.

What would fix it

A specification for the export that would work, not a complaint about this one. Rows needed for 80% power to detect a revenue lift on a single city.

▲
At the current 1,000 rows, power to detect a 20% lift on one city is 21%, against the conventional 80% minimum. Four in five real effects of that size would be missed. A 6,000-row export of the same nine columns clears the bar; adding a cost column and stable product and customer keys turns it into a decision dataset.
